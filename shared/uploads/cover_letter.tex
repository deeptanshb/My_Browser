\textbf{Re: Submission of ``Temporal Attention Collapse in EEG-to-Text Decoding:
Repair with Hierarchical Temporal Pooling and Anatomical Encoding via

Dear Editors,

We write to submit the above manuscript for consideration as a Research Article
in \textit{Nature Machine Intelligence}.

\textbf{The problem and why it matters.}
Open-vocabulary decoding of natural language from scalp EEG during silent
reading is a foundational challenge in brain--computer interface research,
with direct implications for patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and
locked-in syndrome who cannot speak or type.
Despite significant recent progress, every existing open-vocabulary system
on the ZuCo benchmark — the largest open EEG-to-text corpus — encodes all
scalp electrodes as a single mean-pooled vector and applies single-level
softmax attention over long sequences, discarding both the anatomical
structure of the cortical reading network and all meaningful temporal dynamics.

\textbf{The core finding.}
We identify and formally characterise a structural failure that has gone
unrecognised across all prior ZuCo systems: temporal attention inside the EEG
encoder collapses to a near-uniform $1/T$ distribution during training,
rendering it mechanically identical to mean pooling regardless of the recurrent
computation performed.
This collapse follows predictably from the softmax denominator scaling with
sequence length — a property of the architecture, not the data.
We confirm it empirically: Baseline-2's mean attention entropy across
$n{=}160$ validation samples reaches $5.53 \pm 0.01$\,nats, within 0.4\%
of the theoretical maximum $\log(256){=}5.545$\,nats.
The repair — \textbf{Hierarchical Temporal Pooling (HTP)}, the paper's
central contribution — recovers 10--30$\times$ attention-norm magnitude and
produces a 37\% improvement in EEG--token alignment (TF/FG ratio: $6.19\times
\to 4.49\times$), with the corpus BLEU gain standing at $12\times$ the
MC-dropout noise floor.

\textbf{The contributions and why they are novel.}
Alongside HTP, we introduce the first anatomically decomposed Multi-Region
GRU--Transformer encoder on ZuCo — partitioning 24 scalp electrodes into
six cortically motivated regions (Wernicke's area, VWFA, angular gyrus,
bilateral parietal) with dedicated GRU--Transformer stacks per region.
The resulting attention profiles recover the known hierarchy of cortical
reading circuits: VWFA dominates under timed presentation; left parietal
under self-paced reading — consistent with established reading neuroscience
and providing interpretable evidence beyond BLEU scores.
We further release three falsifiable predictions for cross-paradigm
verification (motor-imagery, P300 spelling, SSVEP) and a diagnostic
tool (\\texttt{diagnose\\_collapse.py}) enabling other researchers to test
whether the same collapse is present in their own models without retraining.
No prior ZuCo sentence-generation system employs regional decomposition,
hierarchical pooling, MoCo contrastive pretraining, or LoRA-based decoder
adaptation.
We also contribute the first EEG-to-text system on ZuCo that fuses
simultaneous eye-tracking as a parallel input modality.
An exploratory 4-qubit variational residual projector (QFP, 8,476 parameters)
provides marginal regularisation as a secondary component
($\Delta_\text{val}{=}{-}0.0011$; generation improvement non-significant,
$p{=}0.668$).

\textbf{Why \textit{Nature Machine Intelligence}.}
This work sits at the intersection of machine learning methods, neuroscience,
and clinical brain--computer interfaces — precisely the interdisciplinary space
\textit{Nature Machine Intelligence} is designed for.
The central contribution is a mechanistic diagnosis of a structural failure
present across an entire class of prior systems — one that is architecture-driven
and therefore likely to generalise beyond ZuCo to motor-imagery, P300, and
SSVEP paradigms.
The repair is supported by interpretable neuroscientific evidence,
formally characterised predictions for independent verification, and a
complete reproducible pipeline on consumer-grade hardware.
We believe it will be of interest to readers across the ML, BCI, and
cognitive neuroscience communities.
